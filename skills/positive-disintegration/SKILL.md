# Positive Disintegration

## Overview

Use this skill to decide what should dissolve so the system can keep developing. Positive disintegration is not destruction for its own sake. It dissolves obsolete structures, releases trapped resources, and preserves the useful substrate that should remain in the body.

Identify low-settlement structures, obsolete doctrine, and repeated successful patterns. Require human review for destructive dissolution when configured.

## When To Use

Use this skill when the question is:

- What should be retired?
- Which structure is blocking horizon expansion?
- What should die, and what should be metabolized from it?
- Which repeated pattern should become standard body instead of ad hoc effort?

Do not use this skill as a pretext for novelty, churn, or destructive cleanup without settlement evidence.

## Inputs

- Candidate structures, workflows, rules, or artifacts under review.
- Settlement history showing failure, stagnation, or repeated low-value maintenance.
- Evidence of repeated success worth preserving.
- Policy gates for human review.
- Known dependencies and blast radius.

## Workflow

1. Identify the structure under review and the pressure it was meant to serve.
2. Check its settlement history. Look for repeated failure, stagnation, circular motion, or payment without meaningful horizon expansion.
3. Separate what should dissolve from what should be salvaged.
4. Evaluate blast radius and review gates before destructive action.
5. Recommend one of:
	- keep and continue using.
	- simplify or reduce scope.
	- dissolve and retire.
	- absorb successful fragments into substrate.
6. Explain how the recommendation changes the next horizon.

## Output

Return all of the following:

- Structures recommended for dissolution, retention, simplification, or absorption.
- Settlement evidence supporting each recommendation.
- Salvageable substrate to preserve.
- Review or approval gates required before destructive action.

## Common Failure Modes

- Destroying structure because it is old rather than because it fails settlement.
- Failing to preserve useful substrate.
- Using disintegration language to justify unexamined preference.
- Ignoring dependencies and blast radius.
- Skipping human review when policy requires it.

Use `references/disintegration.md` to keep the doctrine precise, and `scripts/disintegrate.py` when a structured evaluation scaffold is useful.
